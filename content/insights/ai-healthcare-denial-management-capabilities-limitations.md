# AI for Healthcare Denial Management: Capabilities and Limitations

Artificial intelligence is increasingly present in healthcare revenue cycle management — on both sides of the denial. Payers use AI to triage and, in some cases, auto-deny claims. Providers are beginning to use AI to review denials, analyze documentation, and draft appeals.

AI can accelerate denial management. It cannot, however, replace human clinical or billing judgment. This article outlines what AI can do well in denial management — and where its limits lie.

## What AI can do well

### Document ingestion and extraction

AI models can read clinical charts, EOBs, remittance advices, and payer policy documents. They can extract ICD-10 codes, CPT codes, dates, denial reasons, and policy citations — tasks that are slow and error-prone when done manually.

### Denial reason mapping

AI can map a payer’s stated denial reason to the relevant clinical criteria. For example, a “lack of medical necessity” denial can be matched to the payer’s published coverage guideline for that procedure.

### Appeal drafting

Given the denial reason, the chart, and the payer policy, AI can draft a structured appeal letter that:

- States the denial being disputed.
- Cites chart evidence supporting the service.
- Quotes the relevant policy clause.
- Requests a specific outcome.

### Pattern detection

Across many denials, AI can identify recurring reason codes, payer-specific patterns, and high-value opportunities — helping practices prioritize which denials to work first.

## What AI cannot do

### Exercise clinical judgment

AI does not examine patients, diagnose conditions, or determine medical necessity. Whether a service was appropriate remains a clinical determination. AI can surface evidence, but a clinician must confirm it.

### Guarantee appeal outcomes

Payers make the final decision. An appeal package — even a well-cited one — does not guarantee reversal. AI cannot predict payer behavior with certainty.

### Substitute for human review

AI-generated appeal drafts can contain errors, hallucinated citations, or misapplied policy clauses. Every draft must be reviewed by someone with clinical or billing expertise before submission.

### Establish compliance

Using AI does not automatically make a process HIPAA-compliant or otherwise compliant. Data handling, BAAs, and security controls must be independently verified.

## Responsible use principles

For denial management, AI should be positioned as decision support, not decision-maker:

- **Human-in-the-loop.** A person reviews every AI-generated appeal before filing.
- **Verified citations.** Policy clauses and code meanings are checked against current payer documents.
- **Transparency.** Staff understand what the AI did and what still requires judgment.
- **No guaranteed outcomes.** Marketing or materials should not promise specific recovery rates or denial reversals.
