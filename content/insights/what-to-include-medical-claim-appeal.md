# What Healthcare Practices Should Include in a Medical Claim Appeal

An appeal letter is the cover document, but the appeal package is what wins or loses a denial. Payers review appeals against their published coverage criteria, and they make decisions based on the documentation submitted — not on assumptions about what the chart contains.

This article outlines the elements of a complete appeal package for a medical claim denial.

## Core components of an appeal

### 1. Cover letter or appeal letter

The appeal letter identifies the claim, states the denial reason being disputed, and requests reconsideration. It should include:

- Patient name and member ID
- Claim number and date(s) of service
- Provider name and NPI
- The specific denial code and reason cited by the payer
- A concise statement of why the denial should be overturned
- A request for a specific outcome (payment, reprocessing, or peer-to-peer review)

### 2. Clinical documentation

Include the relevant portions of the medical record that support the service:

- History and physical
- Progress notes
- Diagnostic test results and imaging reports
- Operative reports, if applicable
- Treatment plans and conservative-therapy history

Only submit the records relevant to the denied claim. Dumping an entire chart can obscure the key evidence.

### 3. Payer policy citations

Quote the specific clause from the payer’s medical policy, local coverage determination (LCD), or national coverage determination (NCD) that supports coverage for the procedure under the patient’s diagnosis. Include the policy name, section number, and effective date.

### 4. Letter of medical necessity

A concise statement from the treating clinician explaining why the service was medically necessary, why alternatives were insufficient, and what the expected outcome was. This should be signed and dated.

## Organizing the package

Payers receive high volumes of appeals. A well-organized package improves the reviewer’s ability to reach a favorable decision:

- Use a cover sheet listing each document included.
- Label exhibits clearly (Exhibit A: progress notes, Exhibit B: policy citation, etc.).
- Reference exhibits in the appeal letter (“As documented in Exhibit B, conservative therapy was attempted for 8 weeks…”).
- Submit through the payer’s designated appeals channel within the timely-filing window.

## Human review is essential

Appeal drafts should be reviewed by someone with clinical or billing knowledge before submission. Automated tools can assemble the package, but a human must confirm that the cited chart evidence actually supports the argument and that the payer policy is current.
