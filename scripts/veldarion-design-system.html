§ 01 — Brand

The Appeal Brief aesthetic.

Veldarion is an autonomous AI agent service that overturns denied medical claims for specialty clinics. Users do not log into software — they send us denied EOBs, our agents read the clinical charts and payer policies, draft appeal letters, and file them on the clinic's behalf. We are paid a 10% contingency on recovered revenue. Zero upfront. Zero SaaS tax.

The brand speaks the language of a legal brief × medical chart × financial filing — confident, technical, anti-SaaS. The visual system supports this voice: parchment backgrounds recall filed paper; india ink for authoritative text; chartreuse as the recovered-revenue signal; amber for value and the core; oxblood for the problem we overturn. No gradients. No glassmorphism. No purple. No "AI-powered platform" copy.

What makes Veldarion visually unique against other AI SaaS sites: (1) the parchment-and-ink palette reads as legal authority, not as a tech demo; (2) the chartreuse CTA with offset shadow looks like a stamped seal, not a flat colored rectangle; (3) the variable Fraunces serif with optical sizing gives large headlines dramatic editorial character that no other AI SaaS site uses; (4) JetBrains Mono eyebrow labels and metadata bars reinforce the filed-document metaphor.

§ 02 — Logo

The V mark + the signal.

The Veldarion mark is a filled geometric V with an upward arrow rising from the V's opening. It reads simultaneously as the brand initial and as the act of lifting denied claims upward — recovered revenue, won appeals, the signal going up. The mark is constructed from two SVG paths inside a rounded-corner container.

Primary · ink-bg
Inverse · parchment-bg

Construction. A rounded-corner container (40 × 44 units, rx 6) holds two filled paths. The V slab uses 6 vertices: outer-top-left (7,14), vertex (20,34), outer-top-right (33,14), inner-top-right (28,14), inner-vertex (20,26), inner-top-left (12,14). The arrow is a triangle: tip (20,4), base-left (13,14), base-right (27,14). The arrow's base aligns with the V's top opening.

Aspect ratio. The mark has a 40:44 aspect ratio (height exceeds width by 10%). Never distort it to a square.

Clear space. Maintain at least ½ the mark's width as padding on all sides. The wordmark "Veldarion" uses Fraunces Bold at a size matching the mark's height; letter-spacing tight (-0.02em).

Don'ts. Don't recolor the chartreuse to any other accent. Don't stretch the aspect ratio. Don't rotate the mark. Don't place the light (parchment-bg) mark on a parchment background — minimum 4.5:1 contrast required.

§ 04 — Typography

A variable serif, a tight sans, a clinical mono.

Three typefaces form the Veldarion voice. Fraunces — a variable serif with optical sizing — for headlines and display numerals; at large sizes the optical-size axis makes the strokes more dramatic and editorial. Inter Tight — a tight grotesk — for body copy, UI, navigation. JetBrains Mono — for eyebrow labels, telemetry, file metadata, HUD readouts. Mixed italic accents on display headlines give the system its editorial character: regular + italic in a single headline line, italic typically colored in a brand accent.

§ 05 — Components

Stamps, seals, and filed signals.

Every component borrows from legal-document chrome — stamped seals, mono metadata bars, oxblood "Filed" stamps rotated -6°. The CTA is never a flat colored rectangle; it carries a 4px offset shadow that mimics an ink stamp pressed into paper. On hover, the shadow shrinks to 2px and the button translates 2px down — the stamp being pressed.

§ 06 — Motion

Reveal, stagger, and the mask wipe.

Motion is restrained and editorial. Three primitives carry 95% of all animation: Reveal (opacity + y-translation, 0.8s), StaggerGroup (parent with staggered children, 0.1s apart), and MaskReveal (clip-path wipe upward for serif headlines, 1.1s). All use the ease curve [0.22, 1, 0.36, 1] — a sharp ease-out that feels like paper settling onto a desk.

Reduced motion. All animations honor prefers-reduced-motion via Framer Motion's useReducedMotion() hook. When reduced, Reveal returns plain divs and infinite-loop animations are skipped entirely.

§ 07 — Dimension

The Ascent Field.

Veldarion's third dimension is a topographic ink-line terrain — an engineer's survey of the payer-rules landscape — rendered live in WebGL behind the hero. Through it rises a chartreuse ascent ridge: the logo's upward arrow extruded into 3D, its height accelerating quadratically to embody the compounding edge of the rules graph. A signal pulse climbs the ridge every 4.2 seconds — a denied claim being recovered — while three octahedral seals (chartreuse, amber, ink) hover above the path.

Depth never decorates for its own sake. Documents become physical objects: the appeal-letter card tilts to the pointer like a brief held in the hand; the Rules Graph rises from the desk on scroll, then hangs as a holographic control surface; the pricing agreement separates into Z-layers with the 10% numeral and $0 Upfront stamp floating above the card plane. Every 3D gesture is a re-statement of the same idea — the brief is a real object, and it is moving upward.

Layered separation architecture — the 3D scene is an isolated layer beneath the Motion UI layer; the two never share state.

• Terrain: LineSegments grid, 68×42 vertices desktop / 42×26 mobile, vertex-colored ink → amber → chartreuse toward the ridge, dissolving to parchment at the frame edges
• Ridge height: h(t) = 0.3 + t² · 3.1 — quadratic acceleration = the compounding edge
• Signal pulse: eased loop up the ridge every 4.2s, ink core + chartreuse halo, size pulsing at 6 rad/s
• Camera rig: pointer parallax (±0.75x, pointer:fine only) + idle sway (0.07 rad/s) + scroll drift (group and camera separate with scroll)
• Atmosphere: fog to parchment 9→26 units; opacity fades ×0.5 as the hero scrolls away
  • Progressive enhancement: three is dynamically imported after hydration — the landing page renders fully without it; WebGL failure degrades silently to the parchment background.
  • Battery discipline: the render loop pauses when the canvas leaves the viewport or the tab hides (IntersectionObserver + visibilitychange); DPR is capped at 2 desktop / 1.5 mobile; the renderer requests low-power GPU profiles.
  • Reduced motion: a single static frame is rendered and the loop never starts; Tilt3D disables pointer tilt entirely.
  • Cleanup: every geometry, material, and renderer is disposed on unmount — zero leaked GPU memory across route changes.
  • Tilt3D wrapper: spring-physics pointer tilt (stiffness 160 / damping 18), mouse pointers only; children opt into depth with translateZ planes (14–34px) over preserve-3d.
§ 08 — Voice & Tone

Brief. Legal. Confident.

Veldarion writes like a medical director writing a brief — short sentences, technical terms used without apology, no marketing hedging. We say "denied" not "deferred." We say "appeal" not "request for reconsideration." We say "you pay only when revenue is recovered" not "flexible pricing options."

The numbered eyebrow labels (§ 01, § 02, § 03...) reinforce that this is a filed document, not a marketing site. The mono metadata bars ("Re: Appeal — Denied Claim #8472 · ICD-10: M54.5 · Policy: CGS-LCD-L33515 · Confidence: 94%") give the design clinical authority.

  • Do say: "Send us your denied claims. We handle the appeal end-to-end."
  • Do say: "You pay only when revenue is recovered."
  • Do say: "10% of recovered revenue. $0 upfront."
  • Do say: "Our agents read the charts and file the appeals on your behalf."
  • Don't say: "Sign up free," "Start your free trial," "Get early access," "Talk to sales."
  • Don't say: "AI-powered platform," "Leverage intelligence," "Disrupt the ecosystem."
